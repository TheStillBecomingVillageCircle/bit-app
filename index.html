How BIT reviews messages (not AI)

BIT currently checks for a small set of phrases. It does not understand the full context or decide whether your feelings are valid.

  • Broad statements about not being heard or listened to — asks for specific context.
  • Absolute phrases such as “you always,” “you never,” or “every single time” — suggests a specific example.
  • Direct insults — suggests focusing on the behavior and its impact.
  • Threats — warns against sending threatening language.
  • Commands such as “shut up” — suggests asking for a pause or naming a need.

Profanity by itself is not a violation. Passing these checks does not mean a message is safe or appropriate. Your original message remains yours to edit.

The Interaction Laws

Six principles for understanding one another, respecting differences, and choosing how to move forward together.

01The Law of Reciprocal Respect. Respect and consideration must extend to everyone in the interaction.
02The Law of Interpretation. Intent and impact can differ; curiosity and clarification help us understand the message instead of assuming.
03The Law of Consequence. Our choices affect others. We can take responsibility for our actions without claiming control over another person's response.
04The Law of Mutual Influence. We affect one another, but influence is not ownership or control.
05The Law of Compatibility. Understanding helps us discover whether our needs, values, and choices can coexist without forcing agreement.
06The Law of Individual Autonomy. Each participant retains their own choices while deciding what kind of relationship they can build together.

These are BIT's guiding principles inspired by scientific language, not established scientific laws.
